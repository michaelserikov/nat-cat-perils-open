# Earthquakes, tropical cyclones and wildfires for EMEA from open catalogues: frequency, severity and what the data cannot say

Hazard study on three perils outside windstorm, with one method: an annual count (Poisson check, overdispersion test), a severity tail (Gutenberg-Richter for earthquakes, generalised Pareto for cyclone wind and fire size) and return levels with their uncertainty. It extends the windstorm and convective-storm work (`de-windstorm-risk`, `de-windstorm-oasis`, `scs-hazard-eu`) to the perils named in reinsurance cat-modelling roles. **Hazard only: no exposure, vulnerability or loss.**

**Data (all open, downloaded 23 Nov 2026).** USGS earthquake catalogue, M 5 and above, 1980 to 2025 (76,890 events worldwide). IBTrACS v04r01 since 1980 (NOAA NCEI; 4,910 storms, 305,766 track steps; wind is the agency value, the USA value where there is none). EFFIS burnt-area polygons from the MODIS product, 2016 to 2025, attributes only (40,364 fires of 30 ha or more, 10.6 million ha); the land-cover split of each burnt polygon comes with the data.

## Results (`results/*.json`, `results/*.csv`)

**Earthquakes (`scripts/run_earthquakes.py`, five boxes, not political borders).** Mainshocks after Gardner-Knopoff declustering, completeness by maximum curvature plus 0.2 (5.2 to 5.3), b value by Aki-Utsu maximum likelihood, return periods from the Gutenberg-Richter fit. Turkey (box 26-45 E, 36-42 N): 207 mainshocks, b 0.81 (standard error 0.08), 2.4 mainshocks of M 5.3 or more a year, return period of M 7 about 10 years (observed: 5 mainshocks of M 7 or more in 46 years, the largest M 7.8 on 6 February 2023); Greece and the Aegean: b 0.90, M 7 about 8 years (3 observed in 46 years, fewer than the fit implies); Iran: b 0.98, M 7 about 10 years (7 observed, more than the fit implies); Italy: b 1.04, M 6 expected every 5.5 years (8 observed in 46), M 7 about 60 years (none observed in 1980 to 2025, which says that 46 years is short for a 60-year event); Albania, Montenegro and Serbia: b 1.03, M 7 about 76 years (none observed). Replacing the distance window by the Uhrhammer form changes the Turkish M 7 return period from 9.8 to 12.0 years and leaves the other boxes within 1.2 years, so the window choice matters about as much as the b value standard error.

**Tropical cyclones (`scripts/run_cyclones.py`).** Landfall is the first track step over land after a step over sea inside the box; the wind is the larger of the two steps around it (steps are three- or six-hourly, so this is not the wind at the instant of landfall).
- South-West Indian Ocean (Mozambique and Madagascar, 30-52 E, 28-10 S): 126 landfalling storms in the 46 seasons 1980 to 2025, 2.7 a year, counts consistent with a Poisson process (variance to mean 0.82, p 0.80), 41% at 64 knots or more (49 storms), the strongest HARY 2002 at 140 knots. The generalised Pareto fit above 50 knots (66 exceedances) has a negative shape (-0.51), a bounded tail: 10-year return level 120 knots, 25-year 129 (90% range 113 to 133 from a bootstrap over storms), 50-year 133. A bounded fit is a statement about a short record and about the physical limit of intensity, not a guarantee that no storm will exceed the highest observed.
- Arabian Sea (Oman, Yemen, Somalia, 40-62 E, 8-28 N): 24 landfalling storms, 0.5 a year (0.39 in the first and 0.65 in the second half of the record, not tested), 5 at 64 knots or more, the strongest MEKUNU 2018 at 95 knots. Seven exceedances of 50 knots are too few for a tail fit, so no return levels are given.
- North Atlantic storms reaching Europe (any step inside 30 W to 15 E, 35 to 62 N): 109 storms in 46 seasons (2.4 a year), 56 with 50 knots or more somewhere in the box and 17 with 64 knots or more; the strongest in the box OPHELIA 2017 at 100 knots (classed extratropical for most of its steps in the box), then GORDON 2012, ERIN 2025, LORENZO 2019 and IVAN 1998. The count rises from 1.8 a year in the first half of the record to 3.0 in the second half; the observing system changed over these decades and the trend was not tested, so it is not a climate-change statement. The box includes the open Atlantic and the Azores, so these are storms reaching the European Atlantic, not landfalls on the continent.

**Wildfires (`scripts/run_wildfires.py`).** Burnt area of fires of 30 ha or more, 2016 to 2025: 542 thousand ha in 2016, 1,375 in 2017, 201 in 2018, 773 in 2019, 1,035 in 2020, 994 in 2021, 1,292 in 2022, 834 in 2023, 1,457 in 2024 and 2,077 in 2025 (all countries covered by EFFIS; the number of mapped countries grew over the period, Ukraine appears from 2020, so the totals are not a clean trend). Countries with data in all ten years, mean burnt area a year: Portugal 137 thousand ha (2017 alone is 41% of its ten-year total; the largest single fire 67,521 ha), Spain 121 (2025 is 32%), Turkey 90, Italy 70, Algeria 55, Romania 53, Bosnia 46, Greece 43 (2023 is 40%); the coefficient of variation of the annual total is 1.0 to 1.2 for Portugal, Spain and Greece and 0.65 for Turkey and Italy.
- For ten Mediterranean EU countries the 1,304 fires of 500 ha or more come at 130 a year with strong year-to-year variation (counts 2 to 35 for the fires above 5,000 ha; variance to mean 39 for the 500 ha class), so a Poisson count is rejected and a negative binomial is used for the tail model.
- The tail shape depends on the threshold: 0.90 above 500 ha, 0.78 above 1,000, 0.70 above 2,000 and 0.42 above 5,000 ha (90% range 0.22 to 0.59, 132 exceedances). A fit above 500 ha would imply a 100-year fire of about 3 million ha, which is not credible against 96,610 ha as the largest observed fire; the fit above 5,000 ha gives return levels of 93 thousand ha (10 years), 190 thousand (50 years) and 256 thousand (100 years) for the largest fire, an extrapolation from ten years of data, not a bound.
- Annual burnt area in the ten countries from the tail model plus resampled smaller fires: mean 489 thousand ha against 484 observed, 95th percentile 898 thousand and 99th percentile 1.18 million ha, against an observed maximum of 1.0 million in 2025.
- Exposure indicator: artificial surfaces are 0.58% of the burnt area in the Mediterranean EU group (0.49% in all countries), by the EFFIS land-cover split of each burnt polygon; the burnt area is mostly natural and agricultural land, and the share does not say how many buildings were damaged.

## What this does not show
No exposure, vulnerability or loss, and no insured or economic damage. Earthquake boxes are rectangles, the declustering windows were written from the literature as I know it (Gardner and Knopoff; the Uhrhammer distance) and not read from the original papers, no ground-motion model or site effects, no fault models, no historic catalogue before 1980 (Italy's 1908 and 1915 events lie outside it). The cyclone box choice decides what counts as EMEA, landfall wind is a track-step value from agency data that differ in averaging time, and for the Arabian Sea the record is too short for a tail. EFFIS polygons are burnt-scar objects mapped from satellite images: one polygon can merge several ignitions and the minimum mapped size is not examined here; country coverage changes over the period; ten years are a short record for the fire tail, which is why the tail result is shown against the threshold and not as a single number. Fire-weather indices, fuel and fire spread were not modelled. Not an official or vendor hazard view.

## Run
```
pip install pandas numpy scipy pytest
python scripts/run_earthquakes.py     # needs the USGS csv files (see data note below)
python scripts/run_cyclones.py        # needs IBTrACS since 1980 csv
python scripts/run_wildfires.py       # needs the EFFIS attribute csv files
pytest
```
Data files are read from `~/z_data/z_sre/`; the download scripts are not part of the repository (USGS FDSN event service, NOAA NCEI IBTrACS v04r01 csv, EFFIS WFS layers `modis.ba.poly.<year>` with the attributes `id, FIREDATE, COUNTRY, AREA_HA` and the land-cover split).
